### Part 1 : Freedom

Why I Ride a Bicycle ??  
  
I encounter this question a lot. And most of the time, I’m not able to give a convincing answer. It’s not that I don’t have an answer; it’s more like I cannot summarize it in a single line.  
  
So why do I ride a bicycle? Indeed, why? Do I even need a reason? There are tons of texts available on the benefits of cycling. Are those benefits my reason? I don’t think so. Those "beneficial reasons" don’t really come to my mind when I ride my bicycle.  
  
Then why do I ride a bicycle?  

To me, riding a bicycle is very much equivalent to freedom. You can ride your cycle anywhere—on a smooth road, on a rough cobbled street, on water-logged streets, or through a crowded market full of people. Even if a road is out of bounds for other vehicles and blocked by barricades, you can ask for permission from the on-duty guard or policeman. And if "yes", you can simply lift your cycle and jump over the barricades and enjoy VIP freedom on an empty road.  
  
And if there’s a traffic jam? You can weave your way through like water flows among stones. You can go into a forest with your cycle, climb a hill, and if the situation gets tough, you can always carry your cycle on your shoulder. Most of the time, the cycle carries you and helps you cross great distances. But sometimes, you carry your cycle and cross obstacles. It’s like living in perfect harmony.  
  
Of course, you’re the freest when you’re just walking. But you can’t cover great distances while walking. A cycle lets you cover greater distances while still giving you a remarkable degree of freedom.  
  
It’s not just the freedom of going anywhere; it’s the freedom of observation too. It is often said that on a bicycle, you ride at the speed of your observation. You are totally in control of how fast or slow you want to go. You are fully in touch with nature when you ride a bicycle.  
  
You can notice so many amazing things around you. You can ride slowly and observe a tiny snail sticking to a rock. You can watch a butterfly flying beside you. You can spot flowers in roadside bushes that you’ve never noticed before. You feel like you’re seeing more than anyone else because you’re slightly higher than most bikers or car passengers. You can see the flow of traffic moving far ahead of you, finding a one-and-a-half-foot-wide opening in the jam that’s perfect for you to slither through. You can hear the noise of traffic, the voices of roadside vendors, and yes, even some swearing, because you’re not inside a cabin, nor are your eyes and ears covered by a helmet.  
  
You can smell the sweet fragrance of a soil after the rain—or maybe the foul smell of roadside garbage. You can feel the heat, the cool wind on your skin, a drizzle of rain, or the chilling sensation when hot winds evaporate your sweat. You notice the salty taste of Marina's ocean breeze or the spicy fragrance of Chandni Chowk’s masaledar chaat.  
  
Sometimes, you hear a strange bird call and stop to find its source. Sometimes, you’re just tired, stop for a break, stretch your neck upwards and notice stars above you. And you ask yourself: when was the last time you looked at the night sky? Maybe it was during your last ride.  
  
When you’re away from the city, you feel like part of nature. When you’re in the city, you become part of its culture. You are a silent observer, absorbing everything around you, riding forward, toward your destination.
